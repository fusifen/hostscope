## What actually happens during a hosting Black Friday

Hosting Black Friday is not the same event as a retail Black Friday. There are
no limited doorbuster units and no stock that runs out. Hosting is a
subscription sold at a promotional rate for a fixed term, and the "sale" is a
change in how deep that promotional rate goes.

In practice, the pattern across the industry is consistent. Hosts launch
promotions in the last week of November, run them through Cyber Monday, and
concentrate the deepest discounts on the longest terms. Hostinger is no
exception. The standing promotional pricing — **$2.99/mo Premium, $3.99/mo
Unlimited, $7.99/mo Cloud Startup** on the 48-month term — is already a deep
discount. Black Friday typically pushes the same structure slightly further
rather than inventing a new one.

## The historical pattern

Three things recur across hosting Black Friday events, including Hostinger's.

### Deeper discounts on long terms

The discount deepens as the term lengthens. A 12-month term might get a small
extra percentage. A 48-month term gets the deepest rate. This is because the
host is buying four years of guaranteed revenue and can afford to discount for
it.

The practical consequence: if you are only willing to commit to 12 months, Black
Friday does relatively little for you. If you were already planning to buy the
48-month term, that is where the extra value lands.

### Occasional free-domain extensions

Hosts sometimes extend the free domain period, add a second year, or bundle an
extra domain. These add-ons are often worth more than the percentage, because a
domain has a known renewal price attached. A free extra year on a domain is a
concrete saving; an extra 2% off a $143.52 order is about three dollars.

### Bundle offers

Bundles — hosting plus a builder, hosting plus email marketing, hosting plus a
VPS tier — are a common Black Friday device. They are worth taking only if you
would have bought the second item anyway. A bundle that saves you money on
something you did not need is not a saving.

## Why the best hosting deals cluster in late November

The timing is not arbitrary. Hosting demand peaks in January, when people start
new projects, and in the autumn, when agencies and freelancers plan the next
year. Late November sits just before both, which makes it the natural moment to
convert undecided buyers.

There is also a competitive reason. Every major host runs a promotion in the
same week. A host that stays quiet loses comparison traffic to the ones that do
not. The result is a cluster of promotions in a narrow window rather than a
steady drip of deals through the year.

> The comparison base is the whole game. A "90% off" headline is usually
> measured against the regular list price or the renewal price — not against
> what you would otherwise have paid that week. Ask what the price would be
> without the sale, then decide.

## How to prepare

Preparation is where most of the value is, because it lets you evaluate a deal
in seconds instead of hours.

### 1. Decide your plan and term in advance

Look at the plan comparison before the sale starts. Premium has 3 websites, 1
CPU core, 2 GB RAM, 20 GB SSD and 40 PHP workers. Unlimited has unlimited
websites, 2 cores, 3 GB RAM, 50 GB NVMe, 60 PHP workers and daily backups.
Cloud Startup has 4 cores, 4 GB RAM, 100 GB NVMe and 100 PHP workers.

Pick the one that matches the site you are building, not the one with the
biggest discount badge. A deep discount on a plan that cannot run your site is
still a bad purchase. The [plan finder](/tools/plan-finder/) will narrow it
down in a few questions.

### 2. Know the regular price so you can judge the discount

Write down the current numbers before the sale:

| Plan | Current promo (48-mo) | Regular price | Renewal price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Premium | $2.99/mo | $11.99/mo | $10.99/mo |
| Unlimited | $3.99/mo | $18.99/mo | $16.99/mo |
| Cloud Startup | $7.99/mo | $27.99/mo | $25.99/mo |

If the Black Friday page shows a price at or above these numbers, there is no
deal to evaluate. If it shows less, the difference between that price and the
number above is the real discount — regardless of what percentage the badge
claims.

### 3. Decide your maximum total, not your maximum monthly

Hostinger bills the full term upfront. The 48-month term on Premium is $143.52,
Unlimited is $191.52 and Cloud Startup is $383.52. A sale that drops the monthly
figure by 30 cents changes the total by about fourteen dollars. Set your budget
as a total.

### 4. Have your details ready

Domain name, contact details, payment method. Sale windows are short and
checkout is where people lose time.

### 5. Check the refund window before you commit

Every Hostinger plan carries a 30-day money-back guarantee. If you buy on Black
Friday and the plan turns out to be wrong, you have 30 days to unwind it. Our
[refund policy walkthrough](/blog/hostinger-refund-policy/) covers exactly what
is and is not covered.

## What to check at checkout during a sale

1. **The term.** Sale pages sometimes default to a shorter term than the one
   advertised.
2. **The total due today.** This is the number that matters.
3. **The renewal price.** It is shown in the order summary. Confirm you can live
   with it.
4. **The add-ons.** Sale checkouts add extras more aggressively than normal
   checkouts, because buyers are in a hurry.
5. **Whether a code is already applied.** Sale pricing and a coupon code can
   conflict. If the discount row looks smaller than the advertised discount, the
   code may have replaced the sale rate rather than adding to it.

## What not to expect

**Do not expect the price to be half of the standing promo.** The standing promo
is already the deep discount. Black Friday improves on it at the margin.

**Do not expect a specific date.** Promotions shift by a few days each year, and
announcing a guaranteed start date would be inventing information. Treat the
final week of November as the window.

**Do not expect a guaranteed percentage.** Any article that promises "up to 90%
off this year" before the sale has started is guessing. The percentage depends
on the term, the plan and the comparison base Hostinger chooses that week.

**Do not expect the renewal rate to improve.** Renewals are billed at the
standard rate — **$10.99/mo, $16.99/mo and $25.99/mo** for Premium, Unlimited
and Cloud Startup. Black Friday does not change that.

## Is waiting worth it?

Run the arithmetic. If you need a site live in October, waiting six weeks for a
possible extra 3% on a $143.52 order costs you six weeks of a working website to
save about four dollars. That is a bad trade.

If you are planning a project for next year and have no deadline, waiting costs
you nothing and the downside is small. Set a reminder for the third week of
November, check the [coupons page](/coupons/) when the promotions land, and buy
then.
