## The number that matters more than the intro price

Hostinger's renewal prices are the most important thing to understand before you buy, and they are the least prominent number on the pricing page.

Here is the short version. Premium's intro rate is $2.99/mo and it renews at $10.99/mo. Unlimited goes from $3.99 to $16.99. Cloud Startup from $7.99 to $25.99. None of that is hidden — Hostinger discloses the renewal price before checkout — but on a page dominated by a large promo figure, it is easy to read past.

This guide puts every renewal price in one place, works out the actual percentage increase, explains why the model exists, and gives you five things you can do about it.

Three observations worth making explicit:

**The renewal rate is not the crossed-out regular rate.** Premium's regular price is $11.99/mo but it renews at $10.99/mo. Unlimited's regular is $18.99 but renewal is $16.99. The crossed-out number is a reference point for the discount, not a forecast. If you were bracing for $18.99 on Unlimited, the real figure is lower.

**Web hosting renewals are brutal in percentage terms; VPS renewals are not.** A 326% jump on Unlimited sounds catastrophic. A 67% jump on KVM 2 sounds manageable — and it is, partly because VPS intro pricing is already closer to its real cost.

**Cloud Professional and Enterprise renewal prices are not published.** If you are considering those tiers, ask before you buy. Do not assume the percentage will match Cloud Startup's. See our [plan comparison](/plans/) for the current spec table.

## What the increase costs you in real money

Percentages are abstract. Annualised costs are not.

| Plan | Renewal rate | Cost per year at renewal | Cost per year at intro rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Premium | $10.99/mo | ~$132 | ~$36 |
| Unlimited | $16.99/mo | ~$204 | ~$48 |
| Cloud Startup | $25.99/mo | ~$312 | ~$96 |
| KVM 1 | $11.99/mo | ~$144 | ~$78 |
| KVM 2 | $14.99/mo | ~$180 | ~$108 |
| KVM 4 | $28.99/mo | ~$348 | ~$156 |
| KVM 8 | $49.99/mo | ~$600 | ~$312 |

The Premium example is the clearest: your first four years cost $143.52 in total. The next four years, at $10.99/mo, would cost $527.52. You are not paying four times as much because the service improved. You are paying the standard price for the first time.

> **Rule of thumb:** budget the renewal rate from day one, not the intro rate. If $10.99/mo would break the project's economics in year five, that is a signal to plan a migration now rather than discover it in month 46.

## Why hosting works this way

This is not a Hostinger-specific trick. It is how the shared hosting market has worked for two decades, and understanding the logic makes it easier to plan around.

Three forces drive it:

**Customer acquisition cost.** Hosting is a competitive, low-differentiation market. Winning a customer costs money — advertising, affiliate commissions, comparison-site placements. A first-term discount is a way of paying that cost directly to the customer instead of to an ad network.

**Churn is front-loaded.** A large share of new hosting customers leave in the first year. A long discount term with an upfront payment filters for people who are serious, and locks in revenue before the churn happens.

**Renewal is where the margin lives.** Once a site is live and working, switching costs are real: migration effort, DNS propagation, email reconfiguration, the risk of downtime. Most customers renew, and the renewal rate is what makes the business work.

None of that is sinister, and it is worth saying clearly that Hostinger discloses the renewal price. The failure mode is not deception; it is attention. The promo number is large and red, the renewal number is small and grey, and buyers read the large one.

## Five strategies to pay less

### 1. Buy the longest term you are confident about

Longer terms lock the intro rate for longer. On the 48-month Premium term you pay $2.99/mo for four years instead of $10.99/mo. Over the term that is a saving of about $384 versus renewing immediately.

The qualifier matters. Only extend the term if you are confident. The 30-day money-back guarantee covers the hosting fee, but the domain registration is not refundable, and abandoning a four-year plan in month eight is a worse outcome than paying a higher monthly rate.

### 2. Set a reminder before the renewal date

Two months before the term ends, not one week. That gives you time to:

- Get the renewal quote from hPanel.
- Compare it against Cloud Startup, a KVM VPS, and one competitor.
- Request a migration if you decide to leave.
- Actually complete the move without downtime.

Automatic renewal means the default is to pay. The reminder is how you make it a decision instead.

### 3. Check the renewal price before you buy

Not after. Before. It is on the pricing page and in the order summary. Write it down. Then compute what the site needs to earn per month to justify the renewal — if the site makes $30/mo and renewal is $16.99, you are fine; if it makes $5/mo, you have a decision to make in four years.

### 4. Downgrade or migrate if the renewal does not make sense

Sometimes the right answer is neither renewing nor leaving hosting, but changing what you are paying for.

- **Running Premium at renewal?** [Unlimited](/plans/unlimited/) is only $6/mo more at renewal and gives you daily backups, a dedicated IP, a second CPU core and NVMe. If you need any of those, upgrading is better value than renewing.
- **Running a site that no longer needs 100 GB NVMe?** Moving down to Unlimited may be the cheaper renewal.
- **Running a small site that has outgrown shared CPU?** A [KVM 2 VPS](/plans/kvm-2/) renews at $14.99/mo with 2 vCPU cores and 8 GB RAM — less than Cloud Startup's $25.99 renewal, with more RAM.

That last comparison is the one people miss. At renewal pricing, a VPS can be cheaper than a cloud plan.

### 5. Ask support about retention

Hostinger support can sometimes offer a discount to keep a customer who is about to leave. Sometimes. There is no published retention policy, no guarantee, and no reason to expect a specific outcome.

If you are genuinely prepared to migrate, it is worth asking. Be direct: tell them the renewal quote, tell them what the alternative costs, and ask what they can do. If the answer is nothing, migrate. Do not bluff about leaving if you are not going to leave — and do not let a small discount talk you into renewing a plan that is wrong for your site.

## The decision framework

Two months out, run this:

| Question | If yes | If no |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is the site still worth the renewal cost? | Continue | Cancel or migrate |
| Has it outgrown the plan's resources? | Upgrade within Hostinger | Renew as is |
| Is a competitor's intro price plus migration cheaper? | Migrate | Renew |
| Would a VPS at renewal pricing suit you better? | Migrate to VPS | Stay on shared |
| Is support offering a retention discount? | Take it if the plan still fits | Renew at standard rate |

## The bottom line

Hostinger's renewal prices are not a scam, but they are the single most common source of buyer disappointment. Premium renews at $10.99/mo, Unlimited at $16.99/mo and Cloud Startup at $25.99/mo — increases of 268%, 326% and 225% respectively. VPS renewals are much gentler, between 67% and 123%.

The strategy is not clever. Buy a term you are confident about, write down the renewal price before you buy, set a reminder for month 46, and treat the renewal as a decision rather than an automatic charge.
