## The short answer

Hostinger is the best value in shared hosting for beginners, and it is not the right host for everyone. After 30 days running a WordPress site and a static brochure site on Premium and Unlimited accounts, the pattern is consistent: the price and the control panel are excellent, the performance is good for the money, and the limits are real and arrive sooner than the marketing suggests.

This review covers what you actually get, what you give up, and how to tell whether you are in the 70% of buyers Hostinger suits or the 30% it does not.

## Who Hostinger suits

- **First-time site owners.** hPanel is a left-sidebar control panel Hostinger built itself. If you have never seen cPanel, you will not miss it. WordPress installs in about a minute from a one-click button.
- **Small business and brochure sites.** A service business, a portfolio, a nonprofit site, a landing page. These are low-resource, low-concurrency workloads, which is exactly what Premium is sized for.
- **Bloggers on a budget.** Unlimited bandwidth, a free domain and free SSL on a $2.99 plan is a hard combination to beat.
- **People migrating from a slow host.** Free automatic migration is included. You submit a request, Hostinger's team moves the site, usually in about 20 minutes with no downtime.

## Who should look elsewhere

- **Anyone who needs Node.js on the cheapest plan.** Premium does not support Node.js sites at all. Unlimited allows 5 and Cloud Startup allows 10.
- **Stores that take payments.** Not because ecommerce is unsupported — the tools are on every plan — but because weekly backups on Premium and a 1-core ceiling are the wrong foundation for order data.
- **Agencies with client SLAs.** No phone support, and shared CPU means one noisy neighbour can affect your response times.
- **Developers who want root.** Buy a VPS instead; Hostinger's KVM plans start at $6.49/mo on the 24-month term with full root access.

## The 48-month upfront payment reality

This is the single most misunderstood thing about Hostinger, so it goes near the top.

The advertised "$2.99/mo" is not a monthly bill. It is the total plan cost divided by the number of months in the term. To get $2.99 you pay for 48 months in one transaction:

| Plan | Term | Total upfront | Effective monthly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Premium | 48 months | $143.52 | $2.99 |
| Unlimited | 48 months | $191.52 | $3.99 |
| Cloud Startup | 48 months | $383.52 | $7.99 |

Shorter terms exist and cost more per month. The 12-month Premium term is roughly $6.99/mo, so the 48-month commitment is worth about $48 a year in savings — a real number, but not a life-changing one.

The 30-day money-back guarantee takes most of the risk out. If you decide in week three that hosting is not for you, you get the hosting fee back. What you cannot undo is the domain registration, which is registered in your name on day one.

> **Rule of thumb:** buy the longest term you are confident about, not the longest term available. If you would not bet $143.52 that the project still exists in four years, buy 12 months and pay the higher rate.

## Intro price vs renewal price

The second number that matters is what happens when the term ends. On Premium, the promo is $2.99/mo and the renewal price is $10.99/mo. That is a 268% increase. Unlimited goes from $3.99 to $16.99, and Cloud Startup from $7.99 to $25.99.

Hostinger does not hide this — the renewal rate is disclosed before checkout — but it is easy to miss when the page is dominated by the promo number. Two things follow from it:

1. The cost of your first term is not the cost of hosting. Over eight years on Premium you would pay $143.52 for the first four and $527.52 for the next four.
2. The decision point is month 46, not month 48. That is when you want to have compared the renewal quote against Cloud Startup, against a KVM 2 VPS, and against whatever a competitor is offering. We break the arithmetic down in [our guide to renewal prices](/blog/hostinger-renewal-prices-explained/).

## hPanel, in practice

hPanel is the strongest argument for Hostinger over a cheaper cPanel host. The sidebar has Dashboard, Websites, Domains, Emails, VPS, Marketing, Files, Databases, Advanced and Account. Common tasks are two clicks deep: SSL status, PHP version, DNS records, cron jobs, database access.

Two things it does better than cPanel for a beginner:

- **The dashboard is a task list, not a settings tree.** It surfaces "point your domain", "install WordPress", "set up email" as discrete actions rather than leaving you to find them.
- **Email setup is bundled into the panel.** You create a mailbox, get the IMAP and SMTP details on screen, and you are done. On some hosts this is a separate product.

What it does not do: it is not a hosting-agnostic skill. If you learn hPanel, that knowledge does not transfer to a cPanel host. It also exposes fewer advanced Apache and PHP configuration options than cPanel does.

## Performance

Hostinger runs LiteSpeed rather than Apache or Nginx on its web hosting, with the LSCache plugin available for WordPress, a built-in CDN, and Cloudflare-protected nameservers. Storage is split across the tiers: Premium is on 20 GB SSD, while Unlimited moves to 50 GB NVMe and Cloud Startup to 100 GB NVMe. NVMe is meaningfully faster than SATA SSD for database-heavy workloads, and it is one of the few upgrade reasons that is genuinely about speed rather than capacity.

In practice, a well-cached WordPress site on these plans feels fast: pages serve from cache, the CDN handles static assets, and time to first byte stays low under normal load. The problem is not the steady state. It is the ceiling.

Each plan has a hard concurrency budget:

- Premium: 1 CPU core, 2 GB RAM, 40 PHP workers
- Unlimited: 2 cores, 3 GB RAM, 60 PHP workers
- Cloud Startup: 4 cores, 4 GB RAM, 100 PHP workers

When you exceed the PHP worker count, requests queue. When you exhaust CPU, you get a 503. This is not a Hostinger-specific flaw — it is how shared hosting works everywhere — but it means the honest question is not "is Hostinger fast?" but "is Hostinger fast for my traffic?"

> **A practical ceiling:** a cached WordPress site on Premium handles roughly 20,000–30,000 monthly pageviews comfortably. Uncached, or with a heavy page builder and WooCommerce, expect that number to be several times lower. Results vary by data center, theme and plugin load, so treat any single figure as a starting point rather than a guarantee.

We go deeper on the numbers and what the 99.9% uptime commitment actually promises in [our uptime and speed analysis](/blog/hostinger-uptime-and-speed-test/).

## Support

Support is 24/7 live chat in more than ten languages, including English, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Chinese and Arabic. Median first response on English requests is around one minute, which matches what we saw.

What it is not: there is no phone line. The chat is first-line support — good at billing, DNS, email configuration, WordPress installs and "why is my site down". If you have a genuinely deep server question, expect escalation and a wait.

For the audience Hostinger is aimed at, that trade-off is fine. For an agency handling a client incident at 2am, chat-only is a real limitation.

## The bundles: domain and email

Every web hosting plan includes a free domain for the first year and free mailboxes — 2 per website on Premium, 5 per site on Unlimited, 10 per site on Cloud Startup, free for the first year.

Two caveats worth knowing:

- **The domain is free for 12 months, not forever.** After that it renews at the standard rate, typically $9.99–$15.99/yr for a .com. You can transfer it out at any time.
- **The mailbox count is per website, not per account.** On Premium with three sites you get six mailboxes total, not two.

Free WHOIS privacy (valued at $9.99/yr) is included with domains, which is not universal among budget hosts.

## The honest weaknesses

**Weekly backups on Premium.** If you break something on a Thursday, you may lose up to six days. Unlimited and above get daily backups plus one-click restore. If your site takes orders or holds customer data, this alone justifies the upgrade.

**No Node.js on Premium.** Unlimited allows 5 Node.js sites, Cloud Startup allows 10. Premium allows none.

**1 CPU core.** The real constraint on Premium, and the reason sites outgrow it. Hostinger's own documentation points at CPU exhaustion as the leading cause of 503 errors.

**The email cap.** PHP `mail()` and similar server-based sending is capped at 10 emails per minute and 100 per day, on a rolling 24-hour reset. This catches WooCommerce stores and contact-form-heavy sites by surprise. Hostinger's advice is to switch to SMTP, and that is correct — but it is configuration work you have to do.

**The renewal jump.** $2.99 to $10.99 on Premium. Plan for it.

## Verdict

Hostinger earns a 4.5 for what it is: the best-value entry point into real hosting. The $2.99 Premium plan with a free domain, three websites, email and a genuinely good control panel is a better deal than anything comparable at this price, and the 30-day refund window makes it a low-risk experiment.

It loses points for the things that are structural rather than fixable: weekly backups on the cheapest tier, one CPU core, no Node.js below Unlimited, a 10-emails-per-minute sending cap, and a renewal price that is nearly four times the intro rate. None of those are hidden, but all of them are easy to miss.

If you are starting something new, buy Premium or [Unlimited](/plans/unlimited/) on a term you are confident about, and set a reminder for month 46. If you are running an existing store with real traffic, skip to Cloud Startup or a [KVM 2 VPS](/plans/kvm-2/) and skip the learning curve. Check [current pricing](/pricing/) before you buy — the numbers in this review were verified on 2026-09-17.
