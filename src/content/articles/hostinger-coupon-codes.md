## The honest answer about Hostinger coupons

Most people searching for a Hostinger coupon code are trying to pay less than
the price on the pricing page. The uncomfortable truth is that Hostinger's
biggest discount is not a code at all. It is the term length.

The advertised **$2.99/mo for Premium, $3.99/mo for Unlimited and $7.99/mo for
Cloud Startup** are already promotional rates. They are the discount. Anyone who
selects the 48-month term and pays upfront gets them, with no code required. A
coupon on top of that usually adds a modest extra percentage or a free add-on.
It does not halve the price a second time.

That distinction matters because a lot of coupon pages imply otherwise. They
show a "90% off" badge next to a code that is worth 5%. If you understand where
the real discount lives, you can stop hunting and start deciding.

## What the discount actually is

Hostinger's pricing has three numbers per plan, and only one of them is the
price you pay today.

| Plan | Promo price (48-mo) | Regular price | Renewal price | Promo → renewal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Premium | $2.99/mo | $11.99/mo | $10.99/mo | +268% |
| Unlimited | $3.99/mo | $18.99/mo | $16.99/mo | +326% |
| Cloud Startup | $7.99/mo | $27.99/mo | $25.99/mo | +225% |

The "regular price" column is the crossed-out number Hostinger shows beside the
promo price. It is a reference point, not a price most people ever pay. The
renewal price is the number that will actually hit your card when the intro term
ends, and it is the only one that matters for budgeting.

On the 48-month term, Premium costs **$143.52 upfront**. Unlimited costs
**$191.52**. Cloud Startup costs **$383.52**. Those totals are what a coupon has
to move, and a 5% code moves Premium by about seven dollars. Real, but not
transformational.

> Rule of thumb: a coupon is worth chasing when it saves you more than the time
> you spend chasing it. On a $143.52 Premium order, anything under 5% is noise.
> The current code clears that bar at 10% — but the term length is still worth
> ten times more attention than the code.

## What coupon codes actually do

Genuine Hostinger promo codes fall into three buckets.

1. **A flat percentage off the order.** This is what the current code does.
   **`30OFF` takes 10% off the order total** at checkout, applied on top of the
   term discount rather than replacing it. On a $143.52 Premium order that is
   $14.35 back; on a $383.52 Cloud Startup order it is $38.35.
2. **A free or extended add-on.** An extra year on the free domain, a bumped
   mailbox allowance, or a free upgrade on a bundled extra. These are often
   better value than the percentage, because the add-on has a real renewal
   price attached.
3. **A plan-specific incentive.** A code that only applies to Cloud Startup, or
   only to a VPS tier. These exist because Hostinger is trying to move a
   particular product, not because it wants to discount everything.

What codes almost never do is stack multiplicatively. You will not get 75% off
plus another 75% off. The most you should expect is the term discount plus a
flat addition — which is exactly what `30OFF` is.

### What 10% is actually worth

Percentages are easy to misread, so here it is in dollars on the current
48-month terms:

| Plan | Upfront total | `30OFF` saves | You pay |
|---|---|---|---|
| Premium | $143.52 | $14.35 | $129.17 |
| Unlimited | $191.52 | $19.15 | $172.37 |
| Cloud Startup | $383.52 | $38.35 | $345.17 |

That is a genuine saving, and it costs you nothing but ten seconds in the promo
field. It is also an order of magnitude smaller than the term-length discount:
choosing 48 months instead of 12 saves you far more than any code ever will.

### Watch for codes that change your term

This is the trap that catches people. Some codes are only valid on a 12-month
term. If you paste one into a 48-month checkout, the code is rejected — or
worse, the checkout silently switches your term to 12 months, the code applies,
and you pay a higher monthly rate than the term you originally chose.

The 12-month rate for Premium is materially higher than the 48-month rate. A
code that saves 10% on a 12-month term can easily leave you paying more per
month than the plain 48-month price with no code at all.

## Where the promo-code field appears

The code field is not on the plan selection page. It appears at the checkout
step, after you have chosen a plan and a term, below the order summary. It is a
text input with an apply control beside it.

If you cannot see it, check the order summary area for a link that expands
promotional or coupon options. Some checkout layouts hide the field behind a
collapsible row so that the page looks shorter.

### How to check the code applied before paying

1. Enter the code and apply it.
2. Look at the order summary, not the headline price. The discount line should
   appear as a separate row with a negative amount.
3. Confirm the **term length** shown in the summary still matches what you
   intended to buy.
4. Confirm the **total due today** — not the monthly figure. Hostinger bills the
   full term upfront, so the total is the number that leaves your account.
5. If the discount row is missing, the code did not apply. Do not assume it
   will be applied later.

> If the total does not change when you apply a code, the code is not valid for
> your cart. Remove it rather than leaving it in — a stale code in the field can
> mask a valid one.

## The four traps

### 1. Codes that only work on 12-month terms

Covered above, and the most expensive mistake. Always re-check the term in the
order summary after applying a code.

### 2. Codes that reset the term

A related trap: applying a code can reset your term selection back to the
default. You selected 48 months, you apply a code, and the summary now shows 12.
The code worked. Your price went up. Check the summary line by line.

### 3. Renewals are never discountable

No code applies to a renewal. When your 48-month term ends, Premium renews at
**$10.99/mo**, Unlimited at **$16.99/mo** and Cloud Startup at **$25.99/mo**.
Budget for that from day one rather than being surprised in year five. If the
renewal rate is unacceptable, the correct move is to migrate to a different host
at the end of the term, not to hunt for a renewal coupon.

### 4. Codes from coupon aggregator sites that are years old

Coupon sites rarely expire their listings. A code that worked in 2023 will sit
on the page in 2026 with a fresh-looking "verified today" badge. If a code fails
twice, stop trying it — failed attempts waste checkout sessions and some carts
rate-limit repeated code entry.

## What to do instead of coupon hunting

If your goal is the lowest possible price, the levers in order of impact are:

- **Term length.** Moving from 12 months to 48 months is worth far more than any
  code. On Premium the difference is several dollars a month.
- **Plan choice.** Unlimited is $1/mo more than Premium on the 48-month term and
  roughly doubles your resources — 2 CPU cores instead of 1, 3 GB RAM instead of
  2 GB, 50 GB NVMe instead of 20 GB SSD, and daily backups instead of weekly.
  Buying Premium and outgrowing it is more expensive than buying Unlimited once.
- **Skipping add-ons.** Extra storage, priority support upsells and additional
  domains at checkout are the line items that quietly inflate an order. Most
  beginners do not need any of them.
- **Timing.** Late November is when promotional depth genuinely increases. See
  our [Black Friday expectations](/blog/hostinger-black-friday-deals/) for the
  pattern.

## A checklist for checkout

1. Choose your term first, before you look for a code.
2. Read the total due today, not the monthly rate.
3. Apply one code at a time and confirm a discount row appears.
4. Re-check the term length in the summary after every code attempt.
5. Confirm the renewal price for your plan so you know what year five costs.
