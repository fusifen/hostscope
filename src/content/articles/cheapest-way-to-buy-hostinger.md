## The cheapest option is not always the best buy

Hostinger's cheapest route is Premium on the 48-month term at $2.99/mo — $143.52 paid upfront. That is genuinely the lowest per-month rate in the lineup and it is locked for four years.

But "cheapest per month" and "cheapest" are different questions. A four-year commitment costs more in total than a one-year commitment, and it only pays off if you actually use all four years. This guide works through the arithmetic honestly, then covers the smaller levers that add up: promo codes, add-ons, and the renewal decision.

## The term-length maths

Hostinger prices every plan the same way: a long discount term with the lowest monthly rate, shorter terms at progressively higher rates, and a monthly option at the highest rate of all. You always pay the full term upfront.

Here is the structure for Premium:

| Term | Effective monthly rate | Total upfront | Cost per year of cover |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 48 months | $2.99 | $143.52 | $35.88 |
| 24 months | Higher than the 48-month rate | Set at checkout | Higher |
| 12 months | Roughly $6.99 | Roughly $83.88 | Roughly $83.88 |
| Monthly | Highest rate available | Billed monthly | Highest |

The 48-month and 12-month figures were verified on 2026-09-17. Hostinger generates the 24-month and monthly rates at checkout and they shift with promotions, so read those two rows off the order summary rather than from any article — including this one.

The shape of it is what matters: doubling the term does not halve the annual cost, but it comes close. Moving from 12 months to 48 months takes your cost per year of cover from roughly $84 to roughly $36. That is a saving of about $48 a year, or $192 over the four years — real money, but not a life-changing amount, and you have to hand over $143.52 today to get it.

Two things to notice. First, the discount is genuinely large — 75% off on Premium, 79% on Unlimited, 71% on Cloud Startup. Second, the regular price is not the price you will pay at renewal. The renewal rate is lower than the crossed-out regular rate, which is worth knowing because the crossed-out number is not the future.

## When a shorter term is the smarter buy

The 48-month term is the wrong choice in four situations:

**You are testing an idea.** If there is a real chance you abandon the project in six months, a 12-month term at roughly $6.99/mo costs about $84 instead of $143.52. You lose the cheap rate and keep $60.

**You expect to outgrow the plan.** If you are launching a store and expect to need Cloud Startup within a year, buying 48 months of Premium locks in a plan you will leave. Upgrading mid-term is possible but the pricing is rarely generous.

**Cash flow matters more than rate.** $143.52 today versus $84 today is a real difference for a solo founder. The money-back window is 30 days, so the risk is contained, but the cash is committed either way.

**You want a smaller renewal gap.** This is the underrated one. A 12-month term at roughly $6.99 renewing at $10.99 is a 57% increase. A 48-month term at $2.99 renewing at $10.99 is a 268% increase. The renewal is the same either way — but the psychological and cash-flow shock is much smaller on the shorter term.

> **Rule of thumb:** match the term to your confidence, not to the discount. If you would not bet the full upfront amount that the project exists in four years, buy 12 months and treat the higher rate as the cost of optionality.

## Stacking a promo code

There is one promo code field per order and it takes one code. It sits on the order summary, usually behind a small expandable link, and it must be applied before payment — it does not work retroactively.

Practical notes:

- **Check the code immediately before checkout.** Codes expire and change constantly.
- **Read the restriction.** Many codes apply only to a specific plan, term or region.
- **Codes stack with the term discount, not with each other.** You get the term pricing plus the code, not two codes.
- **Do not chase a code into a worse term.** Saving 10% on a 48-month commitment is not worth it if the commitment was the wrong decision.

## Why buying at renewal is the worst deal

Renewal is the most expensive way to be a Hostinger customer, for a structural reason: the discount exists to acquire you, and once you are a customer you are no longer being acquired.

The numbers on the 48-month term:

| Plan | Intro rate | Renewal rate | Increase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Premium | $2.99/mo | $10.99/mo | +268% |
| Unlimited | $3.99/mo | $16.99/mo | +326% |
| Cloud Startup | $7.99/mo | $25.99/mo | +225% |

If you let a term lapse and re-buy at the current intro rate, you may or may not get the promotional pricing depending on what Hostinger is running. That is not a plan; it is a hope. What you should actually do is decide before the term ends.

## The renewal-or-migrate decision

Roughly two months before your term ends, do this:

1. **Get the renewal quote.** Hostinger shows it in hPanel. Premium will be $10.99/mo, Unlimited $16.99/mo.
2. **Work out the annualised cost.** $10.99/mo is about $132 a year. $16.99/mo is about $204 a year.
3. **Compare against migrating.** A competitor's intro rate plus the effort of moving — and free migration is not universal — is the real alternative. If the competitor's intro rate is $2.99 and the effort is two hours, migrating saves you roughly $100 in year one.
4. **Compare against upgrading within Hostinger.** If your site has grown, the honest move may be Cloud Startup rather than renewing Premium. Compare resources, not just price.
5. **Ask support about retention.** Hostinger support can sometimes offer a better rate at renewal. Sometimes. Do not plan around it, and do not be disappointed when the answer is no.
6. **Decide before the deadline.** Renewal is usually automatic. If you want to leave, start the migration before the term ends, not after.

## The smaller levers

- **Decline the add-ons.** Priority support, extra storage and SEO tools are all declinable and none are required. That is often $30–$60 off the order.
- **Take the free domain, but know it is free for 12 months.** After that it renews at the standard rate. Free WHOIS privacy, valued at $9.99/yr, is included.
- **Do not buy a domain you do not need.** Extra domains are a common impulse purchase at checkout.
- **Consider the plan one step up if you are borderline.** Unlimited costs $1/mo more than Premium on the 48-month term and resolves four of Premium's limitations at once — daily backups, a second CPU core, NVMe storage and a dedicated IP. Buying Premium and then upgrading is the expensive path.
- **Use the [hosting cost calculator](/tools/hosting-cost-calculator/)** to see the first term and renewal term combined. That total is the real cost of the decision.

## The summary

The cheapest way to buy Hostinger is Premium on the 48-month term at $2.99/mo — if you are confident about four years. If you are not, a 12-month term at roughly $6.99/mo costs less in total and leaves you a much smaller renewal gap to close.

Either way, the money is in the decisions around the price, not the price itself: decline the add-ons, apply a code at checkout, buy the plan that matches your workload rather than the cheapest one, and decide about renewal in month 46 rather than month 49.
