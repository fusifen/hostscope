## What you actually get for $15.99

Cloud Professional is the third rung of Hostinger's cloud line and the first
one aimed at sites that are already busy. At $15.99 a month on the 48-month
term you get 5 dedicated CPU cores, 6 GB of RAM, 200 GB of NVMe storage and —
the number that matters most — 200 PHP workers.

That last figure is double what Cloud Startup offers. PHP workers are the
concurrent request slots your site can use at any moment. When they run out,
requests queue, and queuing is what users experience as a slow site. If you
have ever watched a checkout page spin during a promotion, you have seen a
worker limit.

The 48-month term costs $767.52 upfront. The regular rate is $44.99/mo, so the
advertised discount is around 64%. Renewal is not published by Hostinger — the
$39.99/mo figure on this page is the standard renewal band for the cloud line,
not a confirmed rate, and you should verify it before signing up for four
years.

### The resources, in plain English

- **5 CPU cores** — one more than Cloud Startup, and dedicated rather than
  shared. The extra core mostly buys headroom for background work: search
  indexing, image regeneration, order exports and cron jobs no longer
  compete with page renders.
- **6 GB RAM with a 6,144 MB PHP memory limit** — double the ceiling on
  Unlimited and Cloud Startup's 3,072 MB. Large WooCommerce catalogues,
  complex page builders and membership plugins all consume memory per
  request, and this is the limit they hit first.
- **200 GB NVMe** — enough for a substantial media library, several
  WordPress installs and their backups. NVMe matters because WordPress reads
  thousands of small files per page load.
- **3,000,000 inodes** — a 50% increase over Cloud Startup. Inodes are files
  and directories; cache directories and image thumbnails consume them fast
  on a busy site.
- **200 PHP workers** — the headline upgrade. Two hundred concurrent PHP
  requests is a comfortable mid-size store during a promotion.
- **300 databases at 9 GB each** — the 9 GB ceiling is the important part. A
  WordPress site with years of post meta and order data can cross 6 GB, and
  when it does you cannot simply add another database.
- **Daily and on-demand backups** — take a snapshot before a risky plugin
  update and restore in minutes if it breaks.
- **Dedicated IP, unlimited free SSL and the built-in CDN** — all included
  and configured automatically.
- **10 mailboxes per website and 15 AI builder credits** — the same AI
  allowance as every other cloud plan.

The website count is marketed as unlimited. Hostinger's own parameters table
puts a technical cap of 100 on cloud plans. As with the tiers below, the real
constraint is inodes and disk, not the marketing adjective.

## Who this plan is right for

Cloud Professional fits the site that is genuinely busy and has the analytics
to prove it. A WooCommerce store doing meaningful order volume, a publication
with tens of thousands of monthly readers, a membership site where logged-in
users bypass the cache entirely, a multi-site network where several sites
spike at the same time.

The pattern that usually triggers the upgrade is specific: page speed is fine
most of the day but degrades between 6pm and 10pm, or during a newsletter
send, or on the first day of a sale. That is a worker limit, and it is exactly
what this plan doubles.

It is also a reasonable choice for agencies that have been burned by shared
hosting. Dedicated resources mean one client's campaign does not degrade every
other client's site, and the 9 GB database ceiling means you are not forced to
migrate a growing store again in a year.

There is one more case worth naming: the site that has already been migrated
once. Moving hosts is disruptive — DNS propagation, database exports, plugin
surprises and a day of your life you do not get back. Paying for capacity you
will grow into is sometimes cheaper than paying for a second migration
eighteen months from now. That is a defensible reason to choose this plan over
[Cloud Startup](/plans/cloud-startup/), as long as you are honest that it is
insurance rather than a present need.

## Where this plan starts to hurt

The first problem is information, not performance. Hostinger does not publish
the renewal rate for this tier. Every other plan in the lineup tells you what
you will pay in year five; this one does not. If you are budgeting a four-year
commitment, that uncertainty is worth resolving with support before you pay.

The second is price shape. The jump from Cloud Startup is exactly twice the
money for roughly twice the resources. That is a fair ratio, but it means the
plan only makes sense if you are using close to what Cloud Startup provides.
A site serving 20,000 pageviews a month on Cloud Professional is paying for
capacity it will never touch.

The third is scope. This is still managed hosting. You get hPanel, WordPress
tooling, email, DNS and a CDN, but no root access and no ability to install
custom server software. If you want that, a [KVM VPS](/plans/kvm-4/) gives you
more raw RAM per dollar — at the cost of managing the server yourself.

Storage is worth a second look too. Two hundred gigabytes sounds generous until
you are storing original image files, video assets or your own backups on the
same account. Work out your annual growth before assuming 200 GB is a lot.

> Rule of thumb: upgrade to Cloud Professional when your site's slow hours
> correlate with your busy hours. If performance is flat all day and simply
> mediocre, the problem is your theme or your caching, not your worker count.

Against [Cloud Startup](/plans/cloud-startup/), the meaningful changes are
PHP workers, PHP memory and database size. If your bottleneck is one of those
three, the upgrade is straightforward. If it is disk space, note that storage
only doubles.

Against [Cloud Enterprise](/plans/cloud-enterprise/), the difference is RAM
above all — 6 GB becomes 12 GB, and the PHP memory ceiling doubles again to
12,288 MB. Enterprise costs $14 a month more. Our
[plan finder](/tools/plan-finder/) will tell you in three questions which of
the two fits.

## How to buy it without overpaying

1. **Confirm the renewal rate in writing before you pay.** Ask support for the
   renewal price on your chosen term and keep the reply. This tier is the one
   where that matters most.
2. **Check your actual worker usage first.** If your site runs fine on Cloud
   Startup outside peak hours, look at caching and plugin bloat before
   spending twice as much.
3. **Take the 48-month term only if the site is stable.** $767.52 upfront is
   the price of the $15.99 rate. If there is a chance you restructure or sell
   the business, use a shorter term.
4. **Use the free migration.** Hostinger moves existing sites at no cost,
   usually within about 20 minutes and without downtime.
5. **Claim the free domain in year one** and set a reminder before it renews.
6. **Re-run the numbers at month 46.** Compare renewal against the
   [current pricing table](/pricing/) and against Enterprise's introductory
   rate using our [hosting cost calculator](/tools/hosting-cost-calculator/).
