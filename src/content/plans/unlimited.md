## What you actually get for $3.99

Unlimited is the second rung on Hostinger's shared hosting ladder, and it is
the plan we point most people toward. At $3.99 a month on the 48-month term it
costs exactly one dollar more than Premium, and in exchange you get roughly
double the resources, daily backups instead of weekly, a dedicated IP address
and Node.js support.

The name oversells one thing. Websites are unlimited and bandwidth is
unlimited, but CPU cores, RAM, disk, inodes and PHP workers are all fixed
numbers. Those are the limits that decide how the plan behaves in practice, and
they are what this page is really about.

The full 48 months cost $191.52, paid upfront. The crossed-out regular rate is
$18.99/mo, so the advertised discount is 79% — the deepest in the lineup. Read
the renewal number before you commit: $16.99/mo is the largest multiplier on
any Hostinger plan, and it is the reason we tell people to think about term
length rather than just the headline price.

### The resources, in plain English

- **2 CPU cores** — enough for WordPress plus a page builder, a forms plugin
  and a light ecommerce stack. Background jobs such as image regeneration,
  scheduled imports and backup plugins no longer stall the front end the way
  they do on Premium's single core.
- **3 GB RAM, 2,048 MB PHP memory limit** — a single heavy page can allocate
  most of that ceiling, so what actually limits you is concurrent requests
  rather than any one page. This is the number that decides whether a
  WooCommerce checkout feels instant or sticky under load.
- **50 GB NVMe** — the capacity matters less than the interface. Premium's
  20 GB is SATA SSD; everything from Unlimited up is NVMe, which is
  substantially faster at the small random reads WordPress generates
  constantly.
- **600,000 inodes** — inodes are files and directories. A clean WordPress
  install uses roughly 4,000. Cache directories, image libraries, plugin
  bundles and a few years of uploads are what consume the rest. On a
  multi-site account, inodes run out before disk does.
- **60 PHP workers** — your real concurrency ceiling, up from 40 on Premium.
  Sixty simultaneous PHP requests comfortably handles a small store and a
  moderately busy blog at the same time.
- **150 databases at 3 GB each** — generous if you run several WordPress
  installs or keep a staging clone. Note that the 3 GB is per database, not a
  pooled allowance.
- **5 mailboxes per website, free for one year** — after year one they renew
  at Hostinger's standard mailbox rate, so factor that into the cost of the
  second year.
- **Daily backups with easy restore** — the single most valuable upgrade over
  Premium. A broken plugin update on a Thursday becomes a two-minute rollback
  instead of six days of lost work.
- **Dedicated IP address** — useful for email reputation and for any service
  that needs a stable, unshared address.
- **15 AI builder credits and 500 AI agent credits** — enough to generate a
  handful of sites and to use the Hostinger Agent for real tasks rather than
  as a novelty.

## Who this plan is right for

Unlimited fits the site that has started to matter. A service business whose
contact form brings in leads, a blog with a real audience, a small store doing
tens of orders a month, a freelancer hosting four or five client sites under
one account. In all of those cases the bottleneck is no longer disk space — it
is CPU and concurrency, and that is exactly what this plan doubles.

It is also the right answer for anyone who wants backups they can trust.
Premium's weekly schedule is the weakest part of the cheapest plan. If losing
six days of content would hurt, the dollar-a-month difference pays for itself
the first time something breaks.

## Where this plan starts to hurt

Two things bite: the renewal price and the shared resource pool.

The renewal is $16.99/mo. Over a 48-month term you pay $191.52; if you renew
for another 48 months at the standard rate you pay closer to $815. That is not
a reason to avoid the plan, but it is a reason to set a reminder rather than
let auto-renewal decide for you.

The shared pool is the subtler limit. Your 2 cores, 3 GB of RAM and 60 PHP
workers are divided among every website on the account. Ten quiet brochure
sites are fine. Ten sites that all get a traffic spike at 7pm are not. Hostinger
does not isolate resources at this tier — that is what the cloud plans are for.

There is also a real discrepancy worth knowing about. Hostinger's marketing
says "unlimited websites" on this plan, while its own parameters table lists
technical caps on inodes and databases. Both statements are true; the marketing
one is just less useful. Plan against the numbers, not the adjective.

> A practical rule of thumb: if every site on your account could survive being
> offline for an hour, Unlimited is enough. If one of them takes payments or
> holds customer records, move to Cloud Startup where the resources are
> dedicated rather than shared.

## How to buy it without overpaying

1. **Buy the 48-month term only if you are staying.** $191.52 upfront for four
   years is a good deal if you keep the site. If there is a chance you will
   quit in six months, take the shorter term and accept the higher monthly rate.
2. **Check the promo code field at checkout.** Current codes are listed on our
   [coupons page](/coupons/) and are refreshed when Hostinger changes them.
3. **Skip the add-on upsells.** Extra storage, priority support tiers and
   SEO tool bundles are rarely worth it at this price point.
4. **Use the free migration.** If you already have a site elsewhere, Hostinger's
   team will move it for you at no cost, usually within about 20 minutes and
   without downtime. There is no reason to do it yourself.
5. **Point your free domain at the account before the first year ends.** The
   domain is free for 12 months; after that it renews at the standard rate.
6. **Set a reminder for month 46.** That gives you two months to compare
   renewal pricing against Cloud Startup and the
   [current pricing table](/pricing/) before the rate changes.
