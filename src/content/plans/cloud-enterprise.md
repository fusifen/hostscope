## What you actually get for $29.99

Cloud Enterprise is the top published tier of Hostinger's cloud hosting line.
At $29.99 a month on the 48-month term you get 6 dedicated CPU cores, 12 GB of
RAM, 300 GB of NVMe storage, 300 PHP workers and a 12 GB database ceiling. It
is the plan you buy when the site is not allowed to be slow.

The defining number is RAM. Cloud Professional gives you 6 GB; this gives you
12 GB, and the PHP memory limit doubles from 6,144 MB to 12,288 MB. On a
managed WordPress plan, memory pressure is what produces the white screen, the
failed import and the admin page that times out halfway through loading a
product list. Doubling it removes a whole category of problem.

The 48-month term costs $1,439.52 upfront. The regular rate is $74.99/mo, so
the advertised discount is around 60%. Hostinger does not publish a renewal
rate for this tier; the $65.99/mo figure here is the standard renewal band for
the cloud line, not a confirmed number, and it is worth asking support before
you sign a four-year commitment.

### The resources, in plain English

- **6 CPU cores** — the most in the published cloud line. Enough that
  background processes (indexing, imports, exports, cron jobs) run without
  competing with page renders.
- **12 GB RAM with a 12,288 MB PHP memory limit** — the reason to buy this
  plan. Large catalogues, membership plugins and complex page builders all
  consume memory per request, and at this ceiling none of them are the
  constraint any more.
- **300 GB NVMe** — a serious media library, several WordPress installs and
  their backups, with room left over. NVMe's advantage is random reads, which
  is precisely what WordPress generates on every page load.
- **4,000,000 inodes** — files and directories. At this level, inodes stop
  being something you monitor. Cache directories and image thumbnails can
  grow for years before they matter.
- **300 PHP workers** — 50% more than Cloud Professional. Three hundred
  concurrent PHP requests is enough for a store during a genuine peak.
- **300 databases at 12 GB each** — the 12 GB per database is the upgrade
  that matters. WooCommerce stores with years of order meta can exceed 9 GB,
  and at that point you are stuck without moving tiers.
- **Daily and on-demand backups** — snapshot before a risky change, restore
  in minutes.
- **Dedicated IP, unlimited free SSL, built-in CDN** — included and
  configured automatically.
- **10 mailboxes per website and 15 AI builder credits** — the standard
  cloud-tier allowance.

As with the rest of the cloud line, Hostinger markets unlimited websites while
its own parameters table lists a technical cap of 100. The practical ceiling is
4,000,000 inodes and 300 GB of disk, shared across every site on the account.

## Who this plan is right for

Cloud Enterprise fits sites where the cost of a slow page is measurable. A
store running regular promotions, a membership community where most traffic is
logged in and therefore uncached, a multi-site network serving several busy
properties, or a business that has already migrated hosts once because of
performance and does not want to do it again.

It also fits the specific case of a large database. If you have run a
WooCommerce store for three years and your post-meta table has crossed 9 GB,
Cloud Professional's ceiling becomes a hard wall. Enterprise's 12 GB buys you
several more years of headroom.

What it does not fit is ambition without evidence. Twelve gigabytes of RAM will
not make a badly built site fast. If your Lighthouse score is poor because of
render-blocking scripts and unoptimised hero images, no hosting plan fixes
that.

There is a second, less obvious audience: businesses that were on shared
hosting, had one bad sale day, and decided they never want to think about
capacity again. Enterprise's headroom is insurance against that specific
memory. It is not the cheapest way to buy RAM — a VPS wins that comparison
easily — but it is the cheapest way to buy certainty without hiring anyone to
manage a server.

## Where this plan starts to hurt

The renewal uncertainty is the first thing to resolve. This is the second
consecutive cloud tier where Hostinger declines to publish what you will pay
after the introductory term. On a $1,439.52 upfront commitment, that is a
material gap in the information. Ask before you buy and get the answer in
writing.

The second issue is that most sites do not need it. The gap between what Cloud
Enterprise provides and what a well-cached WordPress site consumes is enormous.
If you are serving 100,000 pageviews a month from a cached site, Cloud Startup
handles that comfortably. Buying Enterprise is not harmful, but it is money
that would do more good spent on content, design or advertising.

The third is that the plan above it exists. Cloud Enterprise Plus offers 8
cores, 15 GB of RAM, 400 GB of storage, 5,000,000 inodes and a 15,360 MB PHP
memory limit. Hostinger does not publish its price, which means if you are
already at the top of the published range, the next step requires a
conversation with sales.

One detail worth noticing: Enterprise and Enterprise Plus both list 300 PHP
workers. The Plus tier adds cores, RAM and storage but not worker slots, which
suggests 300 is close to a ceiling in this architecture. If your queue is
caused by worker exhaustion rather than CPU or memory pressure, moving up may
not fix it. The fix in that case is reducing the number of concurrent PHP
requests your site makes — fewer plugins, better caching, or a static
front end.

> Rule of thumb: work out your peak concurrent PHP requests first. If that
> number is under 100, no cloud plan above Cloud Startup will change your
> user's experience. Worker count, not RAM, is usually what a busy site
> actually runs out of.

Against [Cloud Professional](/plans/cloud-professional/), the RAM doubles,
PHP workers rise by half and the database ceiling grows by a third, for $14 a
month more. If your problem is memory or database size, that is good value. If
your problem is disk space, note that storage grows by only 50%.

Against Cloud Enterprise Plus, the increments are smaller and the price is
opaque. If you are seriously considering that tier, compare it against a

if you are willing to run the server.

## How to buy it without overpaying

1. **Get the renewal rate in writing first.** This is the tier where the
   unpublished renewal matters most, because the upfront commitment is the
   largest.
2. **Measure before you upgrade.** Check peak concurrent requests and your
   largest database size. If both are well under Cloud Professional's limits,
   stay there.
3. **Fix the site before you buy hardware.** Caching, image compression and
   plugin cleanup deliver bigger speed gains than extra cores on most sites.
4. **Take the 48-month term only for a stable, revenue-generating site.**
   $1,439.52 upfront is justified by revenue, not by hope.
5. **Use the free migration service** — Hostinger moves existing sites at no
   cost, usually in about 20 minutes with no downtime.
6. **Compare against a VPS at the same budget.** At $29.99/mo, a
   [KVM 8](/plans/kvm-8/) offers 8 cores and 32 GB of RAM for less, provided
   you are comfortable managing the server. Our
   [hosting cost calculator](/tools/hosting-cost-calculator/) compares the
   four-year totals.
