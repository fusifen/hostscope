## What you actually get for $2.99

Hostinger's Website Builder is a different product from its hosting plans, even
though it costs the same. You are buying a tool that builds a website for you,
with hosting, a domain, SSL, a CDN and email bundled underneath it. There is no
WordPress install, no plugin directory and no admin dashboard to learn.

The headline feature is that it builds the site two ways. **Manual** mode is a
conventional drag-and-drop editor: choose one of 400+ templates, then arrange
sections, swap images and edit text. **Agentic** mode goes further — you
describe the site you want in a chat, and the AI generates a working layout,
copy and structure for you to refine. You can move between the two on the same
site.

The 48-month term costs $143.52 upfront. The regular rate is $11.99/mo, so the
advertised discount is 75%, and renewal is $10.99/mo. The builder is sold on
the same three tiers as Hostinger's shared hosting — $2.99, $3.99 and $7.99 on
the 48-month term — and the AI credit allowance increases on the upper tiers.

The two build modes matter more than the spec sheet. Manual mode is for people
who know roughly what they want and prefer to place it themselves; it is a
competent editor with sensible defaults and no learning cliff. Agentic mode is
for people who would rather describe the outcome — you write what the site is
for and who it serves, and the AI produces a structured starting point you
then refine. Neither mode requires code, and you can switch between them on the
same project.

### The resources, in plain English

- **3 websites** — enough for a main site plus a landing page and a project
  you are experimenting with. Each is built and edited independently.
- **5 AI website credits** — each credit generates a site or a significant
  build. Five is a reasonable allowance for getting started; if you plan to
  generate many variations, the higher tiers include 15.
- **500 AI agent credits** — these power the conversational agent that
  answers questions and makes changes on your behalf. Five hundred goes
  further than it sounds because most interactions are short.
- **20 GB SSD** — roughly 8,000–12,000 optimized images. Adequate for a
  brochure site or portfolio; note this is SSD rather than the NVMe used on
  Hostinger's higher hosting tiers.
- **1 CPU core and 2 GB RAM** — matched to what a builder site actually
  needs. Builder pages are lighter than WordPress because there is no plugin
  stack loading on every request.
- **400,000 inodes** — files and directories. A builder site generates far
  fewer files than WordPress, so this ceiling is rarely reached.
- **40 PHP workers** — the concurrency limit. Fine for a small business site
  with steady traffic.
- **Unlimited bandwidth, a CDN and a free SSL certificate** — all included
  and configured automatically, which is the practical advantage of a bundled
  product.
- **2 mailboxes per website, free for one year** — after year one they renew
  at Hostinger's standard mailbox rate.
- **Free weekly backups** — a safety net rather than a comprehensive
  strategy. There is no daily option at this tier.

## Who this plan is right for

The builder fits people who want an outcome rather than a platform. A
consultant who needs a credible one-page site with a contact form. A
photographer or designer who wants a portfolio they can update without
touching code. A local business that needs hours, a map, a phone number and
five pages of copy. A founder validating an idea and needing something live
this week.

It also fits anyone who has tried WordPress and found it exhausting. The
builder removes the entire plugin-update, caching, security-hardening treadmill.
There is nothing to maintain beyond the content itself, which for a small site
is a genuine advantage rather than a limitation.

And it fits people who are honest about their ambitions. If the site will
always be a handful of pages that occasionally change, the builder is faster
and cheaper than WordPress from the first day.

## Where this plan starts to hurt

The first limitation is control. There is no plugin ecosystem, so anything
outside the built-in feature set has to be solved with custom code — and custom
code is the one thing the builder is not designed for. If your requirements
include a membership area, a booking system with complex rules, or a
multi-vendor marketplace, WordPress will get you there and this will not.

The second is portability. This is the limitation people underestimate most.
The builder does not export a portable WordPress theme, so moving away means
rebuilding. Compare that with WordPress, where migrating is a plugin and an
afternoon. If there is any realistic chance you will outgrow this product,
that is an argument for starting elsewhere.

The third is commerce. The builder can handle a small shop, but it is not built
for large catalogues. Product management, inventory, variations and order
workflows are all shallow compared with WooCommerce. For more than a modest
number of products, use a [WordPress plan](/plans/unlimited/) instead.

Performance is worth understanding rather than fearing. Builder sites are
lighter than WordPress because nothing loads a plugin stack on every request,
and the CDN and free SSL are configured for you. What you cannot do is install
a caching plugin or change the server configuration to chase a specific
benchmark. For a small site that is rarely a problem; for a large one it
matters, and it is one more reason to be realistic about scale before you buy.

> Rule of thumb: if you can describe your entire site in one sentence, the AI
> builder will get you there faster than WordPress. If you need a paragraph,
> you probably need WordPress.

## How to buy it without overpaying

1. **Use the 14-day free trial first.** Build a real page before you pay.
   Remember that publishing requires a paid plan, so the trial is for
   evaluation rather than going live.
2. **Take the 48-month term only for a site you are keeping.** $143.52 upfront
   for four years is a good deal for a business site and a poor one for an
   experiment.
3. **Step up a tier if you need more AI credits.** The $3.99 and $7.99 tiers
   raise the website credit allowance from 5 to 15 and the agent credits to
   1,000.
4. **Check the promo code box at checkout.** Live codes are collected on our
   [coupons page](/coupons/).
5. **Claim the free domain in year one** and set a reminder before it renews.
6. **Decide early whether you will ever need WordPress.** If the answer is
   probably yes, start on [Unlimited](/plans/unlimited/) now rather than
   rebuilding later — the [pricing table](/pricing/) shows the difference in
   cost over four years.
