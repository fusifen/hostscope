## What you actually get for $7.99

Cloud Startup is where Hostinger stops selling space and starts selling
capacity. It sits above Unlimited at $7.99 a month on the 48-month term and it
is the cheapest plan in the lineup with dedicated resources — your CPU, RAM
and disk are allocated to your account rather than shared with whoever else
happens to be on the server.

The practical result is predictability. On shared hosting, the most common
cause of a sudden slowdown is not your site at all; it is another tenant's
traffic spike. Cloud Startup removes that variable. You get 4 cores, 4 GB of
RAM, 100 GB of NVMe, 2,000,000 inodes and 100 PHP workers that belong to you.

The 48-month term costs $383.52 upfront. The regular rate is $27.99/mo, so the
discount is 71%, and renewal is $25.99/mo. That renewal figure is published,
which is worth noting — the tiers above this one do not publish theirs.

One thing to settle before buying is whether you need dedicated resources at
all. The honest test is not your traffic number but your traffic shape: a site
with steady visitors and a site with sudden ones need different plans, and only
the second one benefits from what this tier is selling.

### The resources, in plain English

- **4 CPU cores** — double Unlimited's allocation and, more importantly, not
  shared. Four cores is enough to run WordPress, WooCommerce, a page builder
  and a cron-heavy plugin at the same time without anything queuing.
- **4 GB RAM with a 3,072 MB PHP memory limit** — 50% more headroom than
  Unlimited and a third more than the 2,048 MB ceiling. This is the number
  that decides whether a large product page renders cleanly or returns a
  memory error.
- **100 GB NVMe** — roughly 40,000–60,000 optimized images, or several years
  of a media-heavy blog. The NVMe interface matters more than the capacity:
  small random reads are what WordPress does all day, and NVMe is built for
  them.
- **2,000,000 inodes** — more than triple Unlimited's 600,000. Inodes are
  files and directories, and on a multi-site account they are usually the
  first limit you hit. This is the most quietly valuable upgrade on the plan.
- **100 PHP workers** — the concurrency ceiling, up from 60. A hundred
  simultaneous PHP requests is a comfortable small store on a sale day.
- **300 databases at 6 GB each** — plenty for a dozen WordPress installs plus
  staging copies. The per-database 6 GB cap is the constraint, not the count.
- **10 mailboxes per website, free for one year** — after year one they renew
  at Hostinger's standard rate. If you are running business email on the plan,
  price that in.
- **Daily and on-demand backups** — the on-demand part matters. You can take a
  snapshot before a plugin update or a theme change and roll back in minutes
  if it goes wrong.
- **Dedicated IP, free SSL and the built-in CDN** — all included, all
  configured automatically.
- **15 AI builder credits and 1,000 AI agent credits** — the agent allowance is
  double what Unlimited gets.

One detail worth knowing before you buy: Hostinger markets this plan as
supporting unlimited websites, while its own technical parameters table lists a
cap of 100. Neither number is likely to bind a normal customer, but the honest
answer is that your real ceiling is 2,000,000 inodes and 100 GB of disk, not
the word "unlimited".

## Who this plan is right for

Cloud Startup fits when the website is the business. A store doing a few
hundred orders a month, a service business whose contact form is its main
lead source, a membership site, a booking system. In each case the cost of a
slow page is measured in lost revenue rather than mild annoyance, and $4 a
month over Unlimited is cheap insurance.

It also fits agencies and freelancers hosting client work. Resource isolation
means one client's traffic spike does not make every other client's site look
slow — and that is a support ticket you never have to answer.

There is a quieter audience too: people moving off a host that has become
unreliable. If your current provider has had two bad weeks in the last year,
the appeal of dedicated resources is not speed so much as predictability. You
stop checking whether the site is up, because the variable that used to cause
the problem is gone.

## Where this plan starts to hurt

The renewal is the first thing to plan around. $25.99/mo is more than three
times the introductory rate. Over a second 48-month term you would pay roughly
$1,247 instead of $383.52. That is not unusual in this industry, but it should
shape how long a term you buy.

The second limit is scope. Cloud Startup is still a managed product built on
hPanel. You get WordPress tooling, email, DNS management and a CDN, but you do
not get root access, custom server software, or the freedom to install anything
you like. If you need that, you want a
[VPS](/plans/kvm-2/) instead — and you should expect to manage it yourself.

The third is the per-database size. 300 databases at 6 GB each sounds enormous
until you have a single WordPress site with a 12 GB `wp_postmeta` table. At
that point you are looking at [Cloud Professional](/plans/cloud-professional/)
and its 9 GB database ceiling.

> Rule of thumb: if your site takes payments, or if an hour of downtime would
> cost you more than the annual difference in plan price, buy Cloud Startup
> rather than Unlimited. If neither is true, save the money.

## How to buy it without overpaying

1. **Check whether you actually need cloud.** Run your site on Premium or
   Unlimited first if it is new. Upgrading later is straightforward and
   Hostinger prorates the difference.
2. **Take the 48-month term if you are committed.** $383.52 upfront for four
   years is the only way to get $7.99/mo. Shorter terms cost meaningfully more
   per month.
3. **Use the free migration service.** Submit a request and Hostinger's team
   moves the site for you, usually in about 20 minutes with no downtime.
4. **Claim the free domain in year one.** It is included for 12 months; set a
   reminder so you are not surprised by the renewal invoice.
5. **Check the promo box before paying.** Live codes are collected on our
   [deals page](/coupons/).
6. **Compare against the tier above before renewing.** At $25.99 renewal,
   Cloud Professional's introductory rate can be the better deal. Our
   [hosting cost calculator](/tools/hosting-cost-calculator/) does that
   comparison over a full four-year term.
