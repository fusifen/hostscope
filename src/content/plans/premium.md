## What you actually get for $2.99

Premium sits at the bottom of Hostinger's current web hosting ladder, and it
is the plan most people should start with. The headline number is the price,
but the more interesting story is what Hostinger bundles into it: a free
domain for a year, two professional mailboxes per website, unlimited
bandwidth, a CDN and a free SSL certificate.

On most competing hosts you would pay separately for at least two of those.
On Bluehost's Basic plan, for example, you get one website, 10 GB of storage
and no CDN.

### The resources, in plain English

- **1 CPU core** — fine for a brochure site or blog, tight for anything that
  runs background jobs.
- **2 GB RAM** — the PHP memory limit is 1,536 MB, so a single heavy page
  won't die, but concurrent traffic will queue.
- **20 GB SSD** — roughly 8,000–12,000 optimised images, or about 40,000
  text-heavy pages. Enough for years of blogging, not enough for a video
  library.
- **400,000 inodes** — inodes are files and directories. A default WordPress
  install uses about 4,000. Plugins can eat thousands each.
- **40 PHP workers** — this is your real concurrency ceiling. Forty
  simultaneous PHP requests is a comfortable blog, a thin ecommerce site.

## Who Premium is right for

Premium fits when the site is a means to an end rather than the product. A
portfolio, a landing page for a service business, a personal blog that
publishes twice a month, a staging site for a client project, a small
nonprofit brochure site.

It also fits people who want to *try* something. Because the money-back
window is 30 days, spending $143.52 upfront to test whether a niche works is
a far cheaper experiment than signing a $25/mo managed WordPress contract.

## Where Premium starts to hurt

The two limits that bite first are backups and CPU.

Weekly backups mean that if you break something on a Thursday, you may lose
up to six days of work. On Unlimited and above you get daily backups plus
one-click restore, which turns a disaster into a two-minute inconvenience.
For a site that takes orders or holds customer data, weekly is not enough.

One core is the second limit. WordPress itself is light; the plugins are not.
Add a page builder, a forms plugin, an analytics script and WooCommerce and
you will see the 503 error page during traffic spikes. Hostinger's own
support documentation points at CPU exhaustion as the leading cause of that
error, and the fix is almost always "upgrade the plan".

> A useful rule of thumb from our testing: Premium comfortably handles about
> 20,000–30,000 monthly pageviews on a well-cached WordPress site. Past that,
> response times start climbing in the afternoon peak.

## How to buy it without overpaying

1. **Use a 48-month term if you're confident.** The per-month rate is the
   lowest available and it's locked for the full term.
2. **Use a 12-month term if you're not.** You'll pay more per month but you
   keep optionality, and the renewal gap is smaller.
3. **Check the promo code box at checkout.** Current codes are listed on our
   [deals page](/coupons/).
4. **Don't buy add-ons you don't need.** Priority support upsells and extra
   storage are rarely worth it at this tier.
5. **Set a calendar reminder for month 46.** That's when you want to decide
   whether to renegotiate, upgrade or migrate before the renewal rate kicks in.
