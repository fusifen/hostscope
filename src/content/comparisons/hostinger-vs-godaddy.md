## The short answer

Hostinger wins on the numbers that compound. It is cheaper at the entry tier,
it renews lower, it publishes the CPU, RAM, inode and PHP worker limits you are
actually buying, and it bundles a domain, SSL, a CDN and email instead of
selling them back to you. GoDaddy wins on three things that are not about price
at all: it is the largest domain registrar in the world, it answers the phone,
and everybody has heard of it.

If you are optimising for total cost over four years, Hostinger. If you want a
brand you can call, GoDaddy.

## Who Hostinger is for

Hostinger suits price-aware buyers who are willing to work through chat support.
It was founded in 2004, is headquartered in Kaunas, Lithuania, and says it
serves 5 million+ customers across 178 countries. Its WordPress hosting is
recommended by WordPress.org.

The [Premium plan](/plans/premium/) at $2.99/mo on the 48-month term is the
entry point: 3 websites, 20 GB SSD, 400,000 inodes, 40 PHP workers, 1 CPU core,
2 GB RAM and 10 databases. The Unlimited plan at $3.99/mo doubles the CPU and RAM,
moves to 50 GB NVMe, adds daily backups and a dedicated IP, and lifts PHP workers
to 60.

## Who GoDaddy is for

GoDaddy suits people who want to buy hosting the way they buy insurance — from a
name they recognise, with a phone number attached. It is also the right choice if
domains are central to what you do. Nobody has more domain tooling, and if you
manage a portfolio of names, that ecosystem is genuinely valuable.

It is not the right choice if you are price-sensitive over a multi-year horizon,
because the renewal step is steep and the checkout is designed to add things.

## The pricing mechanic, in plain numbers

Hostinger's advertised monthly rate is the total plan price divided by the
number of months. You pay the full term upfront. This is the single most common
misunderstanding among new Hostinger buyers, and it applies to GoDaddy's
discounted terms too.

| Plan | Promo rate (48-mo) | Paid upfront | Renewal rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Premium | $2.99/mo | $143.52 | $10.99/mo |
| Unlimited | $3.99/mo | $191.52 | $16.99/mo |
| Cloud Startup | $7.99/mo | $383.52 | $25.99/mo |

The pattern to watch on both sides is the same: a low first-term rate, then a
much higher renewal. Hostinger's renewal is at least published on the pricing
page before you buy, which is more than most budget hosts offer.

> Rule of thumb: ignore the sticker price entirely and model four years. Take the
> upfront total, add three years at the renewal rate, and divide by 48. That
> single number exposes which host is actually cheaper, and it is rarely the one
> with the loudest headline.

## Performance architecture

Hostinger runs LiteSpeed web servers with the LSCache plugin for WordPress, a
built-in CDN and Cloudflare-protected nameservers. Storage is tiered
meaningfully: Premium is 20 GB SSD with a 12,288 KB/s I/O cap, while Unlimited
and Cloud Startup move to NVMe at 20,480 KB/s. PHP memory scales from 1,536 MB to
3,072 MB. Hostinger operates data centers in North America, South America,
Europe, South Africa, Asia and Australia, so you can usually place the site near
your audience.

GoDaddy runs a conventional shared stack. It is adequate for a brochure site or
a small blog, and GoDaddy's managed WordPress product is a better-performing
option than its shared plans — but performance is not the reason anyone chooses
GoDaddy.

One detail worth knowing before you commit to Hostinger: the Cloud plans are
marketed as "unlimited websites" while Hostinger's own parameters table caps them
at 100. You are unlikely to hit that ceiling, but it is a discrepancy worth
noting.

## Control panel and ease of use

Hostinger's hPanel has a left sidebar — Dashboard, Websites, Domains, Emails,
VPS, Marketing, Files, Databases, Advanced, Account — and a beginner can find the
SSL setting without hunting. It is not cPanel, and that is deliberate.

GoDaddy uses cPanel on shared plans plus a separate domain dashboard. cPanel is
better documented and matches the tutorials; the GoDaddy dashboard around it is
busier and more promotional. Both are learnable. hPanel is faster to learn,
cPanel is better supported by the internet.

## Support quality

GoDaddy wins this row on channel alone. 24/7 phone support is a real advantage
when a checkout page is broken and you need a human in the next five minutes.

Hostinger's support is 24/7 live chat in 10+ languages, with a median first
response of around a minute for English requests. Fast, competent, and text-only.
If you have ever spent forty minutes typing a problem into a chat box, you know
why the phone matters.

## Where GoDaddy genuinely wins

**Domains.** It is the biggest registrar in the world and the tooling reflects
that. Bulk management, DNS control and transfers are all better than Hostinger's
modest domain section. Hostinger's free domain for the first year plus free WHOIS
privacy — valued at $9.99/yr — is a nice bundle, but it is a bundle, not a
platform.

**Phone support.** Covered above, and it is not a trivial point.

**Brand recognition.** If you are building a site for a client who has never
heard of either company, "we host it with GoDaddy" ends the conversation faster.

## Ecommerce and scaling

A WooCommerce store on Hostinger Premium gets 10 databases at 3 GB each, 40 PHP
workers and one CPU core. That is a starter configuration: fine for a handful of
products, tight once order volume picks up. [Cloud Startup](/plans/cloud-startup/)
at $7.99/mo on the 48-month term is the practical upgrade — 4 CPU cores, 4 GB RAM,
100 GB NVMe, 300 databases and 100 PHP workers.

GoDaddy's managed WordPress product is the better-performing half of its lineup
and a reasonable home for a small store, but it costs more per month than
Hostinger's equivalent allocation and does not publish resource limits you can
plan against.

One operational detail applies to Hostinger whichever plan you pick: server-based
PHP `mail()` sending is capped at 10 emails per minute and 100 per day on a
rolling 24-hour basis. A store emailing order confirmations will reach that
ceiling quickly, so configure SMTP from the start rather than after the first
customer complaint.

## Which should you pick?

Pick Hostinger if you want the lowest realistic total cost. The
[Unlimited plan](/plans/unlimited/) is the value pick: 2 CPU cores, 3 GB RAM,
50 GB NVMe, daily backups and a dedicated IP for $3.99/mo on the 48-month term.

Pick GoDaddy if domains are central to your work, you want phone support, or the
brand name carries weight with your client.

Whichever you choose, check what you are paying at renewal before you commit.
