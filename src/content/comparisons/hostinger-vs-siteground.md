## The short answer

These two hosts are aimed at different buyers, and the price gap is the honest
reason. Hostinger is roughly an order of magnitude cheaper at the entry tier and
gives you more websites, more storage and more published CPU and RAM for the
money. SiteGround costs meaningfully more per month, and in return you get daily
backups on every plan, better behavior under sustained load, and support that is
widely considered the best in shared hosting.

If your site is a project, Hostinger. If your site is a business that cannot
afford a bad afternoon, SiteGround earns its premium.

## Who Hostinger is for

Hostinger suits anyone building something that does not earn yet. A blog, a
portfolio, a landing page for a service business, a small store testing a niche.
Hostinger was founded in 2004 in Kaunas, Lithuania, and says it serves 5 million+
customers across 178 countries. It sells on price and simplicity and it is
unusually transparent about limits: [Premium](/plans/premium/) gives you 3
websites, 20 GB SSD, 400,000 inodes, 40 PHP workers, 1 CPU core and 2 GB RAM,
and every one of those numbers is published.

The [Cloud Startup plan](/plans/cloud-startup/) is the interesting one. For
$7.99/mo on the 48-month term you get 4 CPU cores, 4 GB RAM, 100 GB NVMe,
2,000,000 inodes and 100 PHP workers. That is more raw allocation than most
budget hosts sell at any price.

## Who SiteGround is for

SiteGround suits people who have already decided that hosting is infrastructure
rather than a line item. It is a premium shared host with its own optimization
stack, mature staging, and a support organisation that resolves issues rather
than reading from a script. Agencies and freelancers who put client sites on it
are usually buying the support, not the disk space.

The trade-off is structural: fewer websites, less storage and no bundled domain
at a higher monthly price.

## The pricing mechanic, in plain numbers

Hostinger's advertised monthly rate is the total plan price divided by the
months. You pay the whole term upfront.

| Plan | Promo rate (48-mo) | Paid upfront | Renewal rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Premium | $2.99/mo | $143.52 | $10.99/mo |
| Unlimited | $3.99/mo | $191.52 | $16.99/mo |
| Cloud Startup | $7.99/mo | $383.52 | $25.99/mo |

SiteGround's structure is friendlier in one specific way: you are not pushed
into a four-year commitment to get a sensible rate, and the renewal multiple is
smaller. But the base rate is higher, so year one and year five both cost more
than Hostinger's equivalent. Every figure above is on our
[pricing breakdown](/pricing/), which we recheck against Hostinger's own pages
rather than trusting older reviews.

> Rule of thumb: if you are unsure whether a project will last two years, do not
> buy the 48-month term to get the headline price. Buy 12 months at Hostinger,
> or pick the host with shorter terms, and pay a little more for the right to
> change your mind.

## Performance architecture

Hostinger runs LiteSpeed web servers with the LSCache plugin for WordPress, a
built-in CDN and Cloudflare-protected nameservers. Storage is tiered: Premium is
20 GB SSD with a 12,288 KB/s I/O cap, while Unlimited and above move to NVMe
with 20,480 KB/s. PHP memory runs from 1,536 MB on Premium to 3,072 MB on Cloud
Startup, and PHP workers from 40 to 100.

SiteGround runs its own optimization stack on cloud infrastructure, with caching
layers and a CDN built into the platform. The practical difference shows up under
sustained concurrency. Hostinger is excellent for a well-cached site; SiteGround
tends to hold its response times better when traffic arrives in a lump.

One Hostinger caveat worth flagging: the Cloud plans are marketed as supporting
unlimited websites, but Hostinger's own parameters table lists a technical cap of
100. It is a large number, and you are unlikely to hit it, but it is a
discrepancy worth knowing about.

## Control panel and ease of use

Neither host uses cPanel, so neither has the tutorial-compatibility advantage.
Hostinger's hPanel is a left-sidebar layout — Dashboard, Websites, Domains,
Emails, Files, Databases, Advanced, Account — and it is the gentler of the two
for a first-time user. SiteGround's Site Tools is more powerful and better
organised once you know your way around, with stronger WordPress-specific
tooling and staging built in.

If you have never run a website, hPanel. If you have run a few, Site Tools.

## Support quality

This is SiteGround's strongest row and it is not close. Its support is routinely
rated the best in shared hosting: fast, technically competent and willing to
escalate. It is the reason agencies pay the premium.

Hostinger's support is genuinely good for the price — 24/7 live chat in 10+
languages, with a median first response of around a minute for English requests
— but chat-only, and the depth of the answer depends on who picks up.

## Where SiteGround genuinely wins

Three places, all of them operational rather than commercial.

**Backups.** Daily backups with long retention and one-click restore on every
tier. Hostinger gives Premium weekly backups only. If you run a store, a
membership site or anything with customer data, weekly is not enough, and this
single row can decide the comparison.

**Performance under load.** The optimization stack is the product. On a
well-cached low-traffic site the two are close; on a site that gets a traffic
spike, SiteGround degrades more gracefully.

**Support depth.** When something is genuinely broken, SiteGround's team fixes
it. Hostinger's chat is fast and helpful but more variable.

## Ecommerce and scaling

For a WooCommerce store, the backup row is not academic. Hostinger's Premium plan
gives you 10 databases at 3 GB each, 40 PHP workers and one CPU core — enough for
a small catalogue, not enough for a sale weekend. Cloud Startup is the plan that
makes sense for a store: 4 CPU cores, 4 GB RAM, 100 GB NVMe, 300 databases and
100 PHP workers, plus on-demand backups.

SiteGround handles the same workload more gracefully under concurrency, and its
daily backups mean a broken plugin update costs you minutes rather than days. For
a store that takes orders while you sleep, that reliability is the product.

## Where Hostinger catches up

Three rows, and they are the reason the price gap is hard to ignore.

**Published limits.** Hostinger lists CPU cores, RAM, inodes, I/O, PHP memory and
PHP workers for every plan. You know exactly what you are buying and when you will
need to upgrade. SiteGround does not publish equivalent figures.

**Bundled extras.** A free domain for a year, free SSL, a built-in CDN and 2
mailboxes per site for a year. SiteGround charges separately for the domain and
does not include email hosting.

**Raw allocation per dollar.** Cloud Startup delivers 4 cores and 4 GB RAM for
$7.99/mo. Matching that allocation on a premium host costs several times more.

## Which should you pick?

Pick Hostinger if you want the most resources per dollar and you can live with
weekly backups on the cheapest tier. Move to [Unlimited](/plans/unlimited/) at
$3.99/mo if daily backups and a dedicated IP matter — that plan adds 2 CPU cores,
3 GB RAM, 50 GB NVMe and daily restore for a dollar a month more than Premium.

Pick SiteGround if your site generates revenue, you need daily backups on the
base plan, or you want support that will take ownership of a problem.
