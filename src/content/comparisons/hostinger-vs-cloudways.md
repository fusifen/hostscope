## The short answer

These are not the same product, and the honest framing is that most people
comparing them are actually asking a different question: do I want finished
hosting, or do I want a server I control?

Hostinger is finished hosting. You pick a plan, click a button, and WordPress is
running with LiteSpeed caching, a CDN and SSL configured for you. Cloudways is a
managed cloud platform that resells infrastructure from DigitalOcean, Vultr,
Linode, AWS and Google Cloud. You choose a provider, a server size and a region,
and you are responsible for the decisions that follow.

For beginners and most small sites, Hostinger. For developers, agencies and
applications with real traffic, Cloudways.

## Who Hostinger is for

Hostinger suits anyone who wants the hosting question closed. It was founded in
2004, is headquartered in Kaunas, Lithuania, says it serves 5 million+ customers
across 178 countries, and publishes a 99.9% uptime commitment. Its WordPress
hosting is recommended by WordPress.org.

The [Premium plan](/plans/premium/) is 3 websites, 20 GB SSD, 400,000 inodes, 40
PHP workers, 1 CPU core and 2 GB RAM for $2.99/mo on the 48-month term. The
interesting tier for anyone reading a Cloudways comparison is
[Cloud Startup](/plans/cloud-startup/): $7.99/mo for 4 CPU cores, 4 GB RAM,
100 GB NVMe, 2,000,000 inodes and 100 PHP workers. That is a lot of allocation
for eight dollars.

And if you want server-level control without leaving Hostinger, the
[KVM 2 VPS](/plans/kvm-2/) gives you 2 vCPU, 8 GB RAM, 100 GB NVMe and 8 TB of
bandwidth for $8.99/mo on the 24-month term, with AMD EPYC processors, full root
access and a firewall you manage yourself.

## Who Cloudways is for

Cloudways suits developers, agencies and teams running applications that need
dedicated resources and predictable scaling. If you want to choose between
DigitalOcean and Vultr on latency grounds, tune PHP-FPM workers, enable Redis,
set up staging and scale a server vertically without a migration, Cloudways is
built for exactly that.

It is not for someone who wants to be finished with hosting by lunchtime. There
is no email hosting, no free domain, no cPanel, and the panel assumes you
understand what you are configuring.

## The pricing mechanic, in plain numbers

This is the biggest structural difference between the two, and it is not about
the size of the number.

Hostinger charges a flat rate per plan and requires the full term upfront. The
advertised monthly figure is the total divided by the number of months.

| Plan | Promo rate | Term | Paid upfront | Renewal rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Premium | $2.99/mo | 48 months | $143.52 | $10.99/mo |
| Unlimited | $3.99/mo | 48 months | $191.52 | $16.99/mo |
| Cloud Startup | $7.99/mo | 48 months | $383.52 | $25.99/mo |
| KVM 2 VPS | $8.99/mo | 24 months | $215.76 | $14.99/mo |

Cloudways bills monthly, per server, based on the size you choose. There is no
long-term contract and no prepayment. You can resize a server or delete it and
stop paying, which is genuinely valuable if you are testing an idea or running
seasonal traffic.

> Rule of thumb: prepaid hosting is cheaper per month and more expensive per
> decision. If you are certain the site will exist in four years, prepay. If you
> are not, monthly billing costs more but buys you the right to walk away.

## Performance architecture

Hostinger runs LiteSpeed web servers with the LSCache plugin for WordPress, a
built-in CDN and Cloudflare-protected nameservers. Storage is tiered: Premium is
20 GB SSD with a 12,288 KB/s I/O cap, while Unlimited and Cloud Startup move to
NVMe at 20,480 KB/s. PHP memory scales from 1,536 MB to 3,072 MB and PHP workers
from 40 to 100. Data centers cover North America, South America, Europe, South
Africa, Asia and Australia.

The catch is that shared plans pool CPU and RAM. A well-cached WordPress site is
fine, but a noisy neighbour is a real risk you cannot control. Cloud Startup's
4 cores and 4 GB are generous, but they are still shared.

Cloudways gives you a dedicated server instance. You choose the provider and
region, set the PHP version and worker count, add Redis, and scale the box up
when traffic grows. There is no neighbour. The trade is that you have to know
what you are doing, or be willing to learn.

One Hostinger detail to note: Cloud plans are marketed as unlimited for websites,
but Hostinger's own parameters table caps them at 100.

## Control panel and ease of use

Hostinger's hPanel is a left-sidebar layout — Dashboard, Websites, Domains,
Emails, Files, Databases, Advanced, Account — and it is built so that a
non-technical user can find the SSL setting without help. It is not cPanel, and
that is the point.

Cloudways Platform is a server-management interface. You see applications,
servers, services, backups, monitoring and scaling. It is well made and it is
not difficult for an engineer. For anyone else it is a wall of unfamiliar nouns.

## Support quality

Hostinger's support is 24/7 live chat in 10+ languages, with a median first
response of around a minute for English requests. It is fast, friendly, and
pitched at beginners.

Cloudways also offers 24/7 chat, staffed by people who understand servers. The
answers are more technical and the expectation is that you can follow them. If
your question is "how do I point my domain here", Hostinger's support is better
suited. If your question is "why is PHP-FPM timing out under load", Cloudways is.

## Where Cloudways genuinely wins

**Resource isolation.** No shared pool, no noisy neighbours, no 503s caused by
somebody else's traffic spike.

**Billing flexibility.** Monthly, cancellable, no four-year commitment. For a
project with uncertain longevity this is worth real money.

**Scaling.** Resize vertically in minutes, or add servers behind a load balancer.
On Hostinger you change plans; on Cloudways you change infrastructure.

**Backup control.** Automatic backups with configurable frequency and offsite
storage, rather than a weekly or daily schedule chosen for you.

**Provider choice.** Five cloud providers and dozens of regions. If your audience
is in São Paulo or Singapore, you can place the server accordingly.

## Where Hostinger genuinely wins

**Price.** Not close. Cloud Startup at $7.99/mo delivers 4 cores, 4 GB RAM and
100 GB NVMe; an equivalent managed cloud server costs several times that per
month.

**Bundled essentials.** A free domain for the first year, free SSL, a free CDN
and 2 mailboxes per site for a year. Cloudways includes none of those, and no
email hosting at all.

**Beginner usability.** You can have a working WordPress site in under ten
minutes without knowing what a server is.

**Migrations.** Free automatic migration, usually about 20 minutes, no downtime.
Cloudways offers a free plugin route or a paid service.

## Which should you pick?

Pick Hostinger if you want hosting that works without you learning infrastructure.
Start on Cloud Startup at $7.99/mo if you expect moderate traffic, and move to a
[KVM VPS](/plans/kvm-4/) if you outgrow it — you keep root access and pay
$12.99/mo for 4 vCPU, 16 GB RAM and 200 GB NVMe.

Pick Cloudways if you are a developer or agency, you need dedicated resources,
and you want monthly billing with no contract.

If you are unsure which side of the line you are on, that is your answer: take
Hostinger. The [plan finder](/tools/plan-finder/) will point you at a specific
tier, and the [SiteGround comparison](/compare/hostinger-vs-siteground/) is a
fairer fight if you want a like-for-like shared hosting decision.
