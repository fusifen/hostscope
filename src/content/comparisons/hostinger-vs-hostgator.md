## The short answer

Hostinger is the better product for less money. It is cheaper at the entry tier,
renews lower, publishes the CPU, RAM, inode and PHP worker limits you are buying,
runs LiteSpeed with NVMe storage above the entry tier, and bundles a domain, SSL,
a CDN and email. HostGator wins on two rows, and both are about risk rather than
performance: its shared hosting money-back window is 45 days instead of 30, and
it answers the phone.

If you are confident about the decision, Hostinger. If you want a longer escape
hatch and a human voice on the other end, HostGator has a case.

## Who Hostinger is for

Hostinger suits price-aware buyers who are comfortable with chat support and want
a predictable bill. Founded in 2004 and headquartered in Kaunas, Lithuania, it
says it serves 5 million+ customers across 178 countries, publishes a 99.9%
uptime commitment, and its WordPress hosting is recommended by WordPress.org.

The [Premium plan](/plans/premium/) is the entry point: 3 websites, 20 GB SSD,
400,000 inodes, 40 PHP workers, 1 CPU core, 2 GB RAM and 10 databases for $2.99/mo
on the 48-month term. If you can stretch to Unlimited at $3.99/mo, you get 2 CPU
cores, 3 GB RAM, 50 GB NVMe, daily backups, a dedicated IP and 5 Node.js sites
instead of none.

## Who HostGator is for

HostGator suits beginners who want the option to call someone and who value a
longer refund window. It is part of Newfold Digital, the same parent company as
Bluehost, and it has been selling shared hosting to non-technical buyers for a
long time. Mid-tier plans advertise unmetered storage and bandwidth, which is
reassuring if you do not want to think about quotas.

It is a weaker choice if you care about raw performance or want to know exactly
what resources you are buying.

## The pricing mechanic, in plain numbers

Hostinger's advertised monthly rate is the total plan price divided by the number
of months. You pay the full term upfront.

| Plan | Promo rate (48-mo) | Paid upfront | Renewal rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Premium | $2.99/mo | $143.52 | $10.99/mo |
| Unlimited | $3.99/mo | $191.52 | $16.99/mo |
| Cloud Startup | $7.99/mo | $383.52 | $25.99/mo |

HostGator's intro rates sit in the same bracket but usually land a little higher,
and its renewal climbs far above the discounted rate. The shape of the deal is
the same on both sides — cheap first term, expensive later — which is why the
renewal number is the one to compare.

> Rule of thumb: before you pay upfront for four years, ask what the plan costs
> at renewal and whether you would still be happy paying that. If the answer is
> no, either buy a shorter term or budget for a migration at the end of it.

## Performance architecture

Hostinger runs LiteSpeed web servers with the LSCache plugin for WordPress, a
built-in CDN and Cloudflare-protected nameservers. Storage is tiered: Premium is
20 GB SSD with a 12,288 KB/s I/O cap, while Unlimited and Cloud Startup move to
NVMe at 20,480 KB/s. PHP memory runs from 1,536 MB to 3,072 MB and PHP workers
from 40 to 100. Hostinger operates data centers in North America, South America,
Europe, South Africa, Asia and Australia.

HostGator runs a traditional shared stack. For a low-traffic brochure site it is
perfectly serviceable, but it does not publish comparable resource figures and it
is not the host people choose when speed decides the purchase.

Two Hostinger details worth knowing. First, Cloud plans are marketed as unlimited
for websites while Hostinger's own parameters table caps them at 100. Second, on
every plan, server-based PHP `mail()` sending is capped at 10 emails per minute
and 100 per day on a rolling 24-hour basis — configure SMTP for a store or a
contact-form-heavy site.

## Control panel and ease of use

Hostinger's hPanel is a left-sidebar layout — Dashboard, Websites, Domains,
Emails, Files, Databases, Advanced, Account — and a beginner can find the SSL
setting without hunting. It is not cPanel, and that is deliberate: it is easier
to learn, and worse documented by the wider internet.

HostGator uses cPanel, which is the opposite trade. Busier, older, and matched by
a decade of tutorials. If you have used cPanel before, HostGator feels like home
on day one.

## Support quality

HostGator wins on channels. 24/7 phone support alongside chat, with a large
support team, is a genuine advantage for non-technical users.

Hostinger's support is 24/7 live chat in 10+ languages, with a median first
response of around a minute for English requests. It is fast and competent, and
it is text-only. Whether that is acceptable depends entirely on how much you
dislike typing.

## Where HostGator genuinely wins

**The 45-day money-back window.** Fifteen extra days is not a marketing number.
It is enough time to migrate a real site, watch it under traffic and decide
properly. Hostinger's 30 days is standard, but HostGator's is better.

**Phone support.** Covered above, and it is the single most common reason a
non-technical buyer picks HostGator over a cheaper host.

**Unmetered mid-tier storage.** HostGator's mid tiers advertise unmetered
storage and bandwidth. Hostinger's limits are published and finite — 50 GB NVMe
on Unlimited, 100 GB on Cloud Startup — but they are real, which is arguably more
honest.

## Ecommerce and scaling

Hostinger's Premium plan carries a small store: 10 databases at 3 GB each, 40 PHP
workers, 400,000 inodes and one CPU core. Past roughly 50 orders a month that
single core is the ceiling. [Cloud Startup](/plans/cloud-startup/) at $7.99/mo on
the 48-month term is the right step up — 4 CPU cores, 4 GB RAM, 100 GB NVMe, 300
databases and 100 PHP workers, with on-demand backups.

HostGator's mid-tier shared plans advertise unmetered storage, which is appealing
for a catalogue full of product photography, but they do not publish CPU or RAM
figures. That makes it hard to know where the ceiling is until you hit it.

One Hostinger constraint that matters for stores: PHP `mail()` sending is capped
at 10 emails per minute and 100 per day on a rolling 24-hour basis. Order
confirmations, shipping notices and password resets will burn through that
quickly, so configure SMTP before launch rather than after the first customer
tells you nothing arrived.

## Which should you pick?

Pick Hostinger if you want the better product at a lower total cost. Go with
[Unlimited](/plans/unlimited/) rather than Premium if you want daily backups, a
dedicated IP, NVMe storage and Node.js support; it is a dollar a month more.

Pick HostGator if the 45-day window or phone support is worth more to you than
the price difference, or if you are already comfortable in cPanel.
